P1 Issues (Fix Before or Shortly After Deploy)

P1 All 17 area page meta descriptions are templated
Only the city name differs: "Best HVAC service in [City], TN. Top-rated AC repair, furnace & heater repair, heat pumps, emergency 24/7. Licensed & insured. Call (615) 219-9882." Google treats these as near-duplicate signals. Each city should have a unique description incorporating local context (county, neighborhoods, distance).
P1 Flat heading hierarchy on all service pages
Every service page has 12 H2 tags and only 1 H3. Should use proper H2→H3 nesting. Example: ac-repair.html has "AC Not Cooling," "AC Running but Warm Air," etc. as H2s — these should be H3s under a parent H2 like "Common AC Problems."
P1 No guarantee or warranty content anywhere
Zero mentions of "guarantee" or "warranty" across all pages. Competitors offer explicit guarantees: One Hour has "2-year materials, labor, and craftsmanship guarantee." This is a trust signal gap that hurts conversion and reduces AI answer eligibility.
P1 No real customer reviews or testimonials on any page
Only 2 generic review references on the homepage, 1 on service pages. No actual review text, star ratings, or customer names. Competitors display reviews prominently. Google's review snippet eligibility requires visible review content matching schema.
P0 Area pages critically thin (390-425 words)
Area pages average only 390-425 words — well below the competitive threshold of 1,500-2,000 words. Service pages are also thin at 640-810 words. Google is unlikely to rank these as authoritative local landing pages. The local content is templated — neighborhoods and zip codes are inserted but there's no genuine local differentiation (climate specifics, common system types, local utility programs, permit details). These need at minimum 800-1,200 words with unique local context per city.
P1 No blog or informational content
Zero blog posts or educational articles. Competitors (Happy Hiller, Lee Company) have blog content supporting informational keywords. This limits ability to capture top-of-funnel traffic and earn backlinks.
P1 No financing mention in the rebuild
Homepage links to /financing (which doesn't exist). All top competitors offer financing. Missing financing info hurts conversion on high-ticket services (AC installation, furnace replacement).
P1 No license number displayed
Pages mention "Licensed & Insured" but display no actual license number. Competitors like One Hour display License 210374. Visible license numbers increase trust and are a ranking signal for Google's EEAT evaluation.
P1 Page sizes are large (65-105KB HTML)
Homepage is 105KB of HTML. Service pages are ~71KB. Area pages are ~67KB. These are large for static HTML — primarily due to inline CSS duplication across pages. Consider extracting shared CSS to a single file for cache efficiency.

What Actually Matters for AI Citations

Based on current AEO research (Elevarus, US Tech Automations, 5W PR AI Visibility Index), the factors that drive AI citations for local service businesses, in order of impact:

  1. Entity resolution across trusted sources. AI engines cite businesses they can confidently identify. Hughes has near-zero presence across directories, reviews, and citations. This is the #1 blocker.
  2. Review volume and recency. Per BrightLocal, 74% of consumers only trust reviews from the last 3 months. AI engines weight recency similarly. Hughes has zero reviews.
  3. Question-led headings with direct answers. Hughes' FAQ sections exist but the answers should lead with a concise 1-2 sentence answer before expanding. Currently the FAQ answers are too conversational for AI extraction.
  4. Specific, verifiable figures. Hughes' pricing transparency ($89 diagnostic, $150/hr) is genuinely strong here. Competitors are opaque. This is the easiest AEO win to leverage.
  5. Structured data matching visible content. Hughes' schema is comprehensive but area pages are missing hours and geo — AI engines cross-check schema against page content.

Honest Assessment

llms.txt and schema are necessary but not sufficient
Hughes has a genuine first-mover advantage — no Nashville HVAC competitor has JSON-LD schema or llms.txt. But the 5W AI Visibility Index reports that 87% of independent HVAC contractors have effectively zero AI citation share. The technical markup creates eligibility; the authority signals (reviews, citations, backlinks) create selection. Hughes has the eligibility layer but almost none of the authority layer. Fix Phase 3 (Local SEO) before investing more in AEO-specific tactics.
